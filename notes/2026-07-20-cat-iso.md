## Why J dies

The old `Cat.Iso` had two J-shaped cells and both were J-shaped
by habit, not necessity:

- **`idtoiso` is a transp-filler package, not an eliminator.**
  `to = coe01 (λ i → hom x (p i)) (idn x)` with its filler
  `to-fill : PathP (λ i → hom x (p i)) (idn x) to` kept
  first-class (`coe-filler` — the `↝̂-fill` discipline: fil cap
  at `i0`, com at `i1`). Dually `from`/`from-fill` along
  `λ i → hom (p i) x`. The old disease — `idtoiso refl` reducing
  only propositionally, patched by `idtoiso-refl-hom` — has no
  analogue: no law ever eliminates at `refl`, because every law
  is a line.
- **The inverse laws are line-whiskering plus one com.**
  `to ⨾ from ≡ idn x` is `sym (λ i → to-fill i ⨾ from-fill i)`
  into the unit law — the pointwise composite runs in the
  *constant* family `hom x x`. `from ⨾ to ≡ idn y` compares
  `λ i → from-fill i ⨾ to-fill i` against the identity diagonal
  `λ i → idn (p i)` over `λ i → hom (p i) (p i)`: two PathPs
  over one family, `i0`-ends joined by `unitl`, closed by
  `pathp-ends`.
- **`hom-PathP→square` is the same two-line com.** For
  `P : PathP (λ i → hom (p i) (q i)) f g`, the whiskers
  `λ i → f ⨾ to-fill(q) i` and `λ i → to-fill(p) i ⨾ P i` both
  run in `hom x (q i)`; `unitr f ∙ sym (unitl f)` joins the
  `i0`-ends and `pathp-ends` yields the classical square
  `f ⨾ to(q) ≡ to(p) ⨾ g`. The old `≡`-of-types form (double J)
  is presentation-comparison material for a Properties module,
  not the spine cell — same pattern as the H2 consumer story.

The one new Core cell: **`pathp-ends`** — two PathPs over one
line whose `i0`-ends are identified have identified `i1`-ends;
the com over the shared family with the two lines as walls and
the identification as base. Four lines, home `Core.Kan` beside
the `comp-pathp` family (name open to bikeshed there).
