# Virtual bicategory formulations in kitcat

Comparative analysis of the ternary composition formulations explored
in this library. The goal is to formalize wild (untruncated) higher
categories via a ternary composition primitive `emb`.

## Background

### Virtual bicategories

A **virtual bicategory** (Cruttwell-Shulman, Sterling) has objects,
morphisms, and a ternary embedding:

```
emb : hom x y → ∀ w → hom w x → ∀ z → hom y z → hom w z
```

Both composites and identities are determined as universal properties:
contractible fibers of `emb`. This makes them propositional — uniquely
determined by the structure, not equipped.

Two binary projections:

- `noy f z h = emb f _ idn z h` (precomposition face)
- `yon f w g = emb f w g _ idn` (postcomposition face)

The bimodule analysis (see `notes/bimodule.md`) shows `emb` sends
each morphism to its bimodule action over two total spaces
`Σ w, hom w x` and `Σ v, hom y v`. When both are contractible (path
groupoids), `emb` is an equivalence. When hom spaces are propositions
(posets), `emb` is trivially an equivalence. In general `emb` is only
an embedding (contractible fibers over its image).

### Braids, 2-torsion, and the identity obstruction

The identity uniqueness obstruction is directly connected to the
braided/symmetric distinction in monoidal categories.

In a **symmetric** monoidal category, the braiding σ satisfies σ² = id
(2-torsion). In a **braided** monoidal category, σ² ≠ id in general.
The braid group Bₙ is torsion-free — no element squares to the
identity except the identity itself. The symmetric group Sₙ is
generated by 2-torsion elements (transpositions).

Geometrically, a 2-torsion element in π₁ is a half-twist: a loop
that becomes contractible when traversed twice. The Möbius band is
the neighborhood of such a loop in RP².

The connection to our formulations:

- **2-torsion-free loop space** → identity unique → Cat.Virtual =
  genuine virtual bicategory → "braid-like" (no non-trivial
  self-inverse loops)
- **2-torsion in loop space** (K(ℤ/2,1), RP²) → identity ambiguous
  up to involutions → Cat.Virtual admits twisted compositions →
  "symmetric-like" (transposition elements create alternative
  identity choices)

The obstruction to Cat.Virtual being a virtual bicategory in the
strict sense is precisely the obstruction to the loop space being
"braid-like" rather than "permutation-like" at the 2-torsion level.

## The active formulation

### Cat.Virtual — ternary embedding with chosen units

**Module**: `src/Cat/Virtual.lagda.md`

**Primitives**: `emb`, `unit` (bare Σ), `compose-contr` (noy-only),
`interchange` (separate field)

**Record fields**:

```
unit : Σ e ∶ hom x x
     , ( (∀ {z} → is-equiv (λ h → emb e x e z h))
       × (∀ {w} → is-equiv (λ g → emb e w g x e)))
     × (∀ {z} h → emb e x e z (emb e x e z h) ≡ emb e x e z h)
     × (∀ {w} g → emb e w (emb e w g x e) x e ≡ emb e w g x e)

compose-contr : is-contr (Σ s, emb s ≡ λ w a v b → emb f w a v (noy g v b))

interchange : emb f w a v (noy g v b) ≡ emb g w (yon f w a) v b
```

The identity `idn = unit .fst`. Absorption laws `absorb-l`, `absorb-r`
derived via `equiv→lc` applied to idempotency conditions.

**Identity**: structure (chosen, not unique). The equivalence +
idempotency conditions reduce to `e² ≡ refl` in path groupoids, so
any involution in the loop space satisfies them. The ℤ/2 generator in
K(ℤ/2, 1) is a counterexample to uniqueness.

**Composites**: property (contractible fibers of `emb`).

**Coherences from base**:
- `unitl`, `unitr`, `assoc`: provable
- Pentagon (full Mac Lane): provable — all five face identifications
- Triangle (weak): provable — `α₂₃` edge remains abstract
- Triangle (full Mac Lane): requires `2-coherent` record with
  `absorb-coh`

**Additional structure**: `op`, `op-invol`, `2-coherent` record

**Instances**:
- `Cat.Groupoid`: path groupoid on any type, using
  `yon-unbiased.emb-equiv`. `compose-contr` via `eqv-fibers`.
  `interchange` via `pcom-split-l ∙ pcom-leftright ∙ sym pcom-split-r`.
- `Cat.Walking`: the interval category **2** (walking morphism).
  `ob = Bool`, `hom` by case split. All fields trivial (propositions).

**Hierarchy position**: sits between fc-multicategories (Leinster; no
unit requirement) and virtual bicategories (Cruttwell-Shulman,
Sterling; both units and composites as properties). Collapses to a
virtual bicategory when the loop space is 2-torsion-free.

## The alternative formulation

### Cat.VirtualAlt — representable decomposition

**Module**: `src/Cat/VirtualAlt.lagda.md`

The key structural choice: `noy` and `yon` (the representable functor
faces) are primitive, and `emb` is derived as `emb f w a v b = noy
(yon f w a) v b`.

**Primitives**: `noy`, `yon`, `interchange` (binary), `noy-assoc`,
`yon-assoc`, `unit` (yon-idempotent), `compose-contr` (both
characterizations bundled)

**Record fields**:

```
noy : hom x y → ∀ z → hom y z → hom x z
yon : hom x y → ∀ w → hom w x → hom w y
interchange : yon (noy f v b) w a ≡ noy (yon f w a) v b
noy-assoc : noy f v (noy g v h) ≡ noy (noy f z g) v h
yon-assoc : yon g w (yon f w a) ≡ yon (yon g x f) w a

unit : Σ e, (yon e x e ≡ e) × (∀ {z} → is-equiv (noy e z))
                              × (∀ {w} → is-equiv (yon e w))

compose-contr : is-contr (Σ s, (emb s ≡ noy-target) × (emb s ≡ yon-target))
```

**Identity**: property (unique). Yon-idempotency `yon e x e ≡ e`
computes as `e ∙ e ≡ e` in groupoids, forcing `e ≡ refl` (right-cancel
`e`). The Kraus argument gives `unit-is-prop`.

**Composites**: property (contractible fibers of derived `emb`).

**Coherences from base**: `unitl`, `unitr`, `assoc` provable.
Pentagon **incomplete** — `face₃₅` requires `noy-composite-coh` from
`2-coherent`. Triangle weak from base; full requires `2-coherent`
with `noy-absorb-coh`.

**The fundamental trade-off**: VirtualAlt gets unique identity but
loses the full pentagon from the base axioms. `emb` being derived
means its fiber contractibility doesn't give free access to the
coherence tower. The face₃₅ pentagon edge requires a coherence
between `noy-composite` and `assoc` that isn't derivable from the
base.

**No `op`, no functors, no instances.**

## Archived experiments

The following formulations were explored during development and are
preserved in `Stash/Cat/Virtual/`. They are superseded by Cat.Virtual
but document the design space.

### Contractible-unit variant

**Archive**: `Stash/Cat/Virtual/Contractible-Unit.lagda.md`
(was `Cat.VirtualLegacy`)

Same structure as Cat.Virtual but with `is-contr`-wrapped absorption
laws for identity:

```
idn-contr : is-contr (Σ e ∶ hom x x
              , (∀ {w} g → emb e w g x e ≡ g)
              × (∀ {z} h → emb e x e z h ≡ h))
```

Identity nominally a property (contractibility), but `is-contr` is
not provable for path groupoids: absorption conditions reduce to
`e² ≡ refl`, same as Cat.Virtual's squaring obstruction. The
`idn-contr .paths` proof attempts in the file document the failed
strategy. Full pentagon provable. `op`, `op-invol`, `functor`
defined. Path groupoid instance attempted but has holes.

This was the direct predecessor to Cat.Virtual — the unprovable
`idn-contr` motivated weakening to a bare `unit` Σ.

### Bundled variant

**Archive**: `Stash/Cat/Virtual/Bundled.lagda.md`
(was the original `Cat.Virtual`)

Same as Contractible-unit but `compose-contr` bundles both noy and
yon characterizations:

```
compose-contr : is-contr (Σ s, (emb s ≡ noy-target) × (emb s ≡ yon-target))
```

`interchange` is derived from the two characterizations, not a field.
Has the **1-type obstruction**: the agreement path `noy-target ≡
yon-target` must be unique, requiring function types into hom spaces
to be sets — i.e., `ob` a 1-type. Path groupoid on S² is a
counterexample. Splitting into separate `compose-contr` +
`interchange` (as Cat.Virtual does) eliminates this obstruction.

Full pentagon provable. `op`, `op-invol` defined. Same `idn-contr`
obstruction as Contractible-unit.

### Coherent variant

**Archive**: `Stash/Cat/Virtual/Bundled-Coherent.lagda.md.bak`
(was `Cat.Virtual2`)

Same as Contractible-unit but `absorb-coh` bundled into the record:

```
absorb-coh : absorb-l (noy f v b)
           ≡ interchange idn f _ idn v b
             ∙ ap (λ t → emb f _ t v b) (absorb-r idn)
```

Full triangle provable from `Cat` directly (no `2-coherent`).
Pentagon not developed. No `op`, no functors. WIP file — explored
whether bundling `absorb-coh` was desirable.

### Coherent-bundled variant

**Archive**: `Stash/Cat/Virtual/Coherent-Bundled.lagda.md.bak`
(was `Cat.Virtual3`)

Combines the bundled `compose-contr` (both characterizations) with
bundled `absorb-coh`. Interchange derived. Full triangle from base.
Pentagon not developed. WIP file.

Inherits both the 1-type obstruction (bundled compose-contr) and the
stronger axiom burden (absorb-coh in record).

axis along which they most differ from the canonical formulation:

## The two obstructions

### 1-type obstruction (bundled compose-contr)

When `compose-contr` bundles both noy and yon characterizations in
one contractible fiber, the agreement path `noy-target ≡ yon-target`
must be unique. This requires the function type `∀ w → hom w x →
∀ v → hom y v → hom w v` to be a set, which holds when `hom` is a
set — i.e., `ob` is a 1-type. The path groupoid on S² is a
counterexample.

Cat.Virtual avoids this by splitting `compose-contr` (noy-only) from
`interchange` (separate field).

### Squaring obstruction (unit uniqueness)

All formulations with `emb` primitive have identity conditions that
reduce to `e² ≡ refl` in path groupoids: any involution satisfies
them. K(ℤ/2, 1) is the sharpest counterexample.

VirtualAlt avoids this via binary yon-idempotency `yon e x e ≡ e`,
which gives `e² ≡ e`, forcing `e ≡ refl` in groupoids. But VirtualAlt
derives `emb`, losing the free pentagon.

### The impossibility

No formulation achieves all three of:
1. Unique identity
2. Full pentagon from base axioms
3. Path groupoids on arbitrary types

This appears to be an inherent tension. Cat.Virtual optimizes for (2)
and (3) at the cost of (1). VirtualAlt optimizes for (1) and (3) at
the cost of (2).

The tension is directly analogous to the braided/symmetric
distinction: the braid group is torsion-free (no identity ambiguity)
but carries more structure (coherences not freely derivable); the
symmetric group has 2-torsion (identity ambiguity) but is simpler
(coherences collapse).
