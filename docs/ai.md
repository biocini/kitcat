# Research provenance, documentation, and AI policy

_by Lane Biocini_

Written and edited by hand, and last updated on 2026-07-05.

This repository is developed with substantial AI assistance under my direction
and review, and aims to be an exemplar of transparent AI-assisted mathematics
research. Truth be told, I'm still figuring out what that means. As such, I see
this project in part as a living entry into the conversation about ethical
practice in this domain. Here are the relevant factors I consider, and a
description of my current approach:

- I will make every effort to ensure that the library is properly documented and
  that authorship of ideas is correctly attributed; particularly because many of
  the ideas explored in this library are my authentic and original work.
  Autogenerated proofs carry the danger of drawing upon work that is not properly
  credited, and may be difficult to trace back to its original source. Just the
  same as I would expect credit for my own ideas, it is imperative that the work
  that inspired me and which I have built on the shoulders of is recognized and
  credited appropriately. This commitment is ongoing: the library began as my
  own informal blackboard, so there is an attribution debt to clear as I polish
  it for wider publication.
- I have in general made the effort to be clear when a given construction is
  derived from a specific source, and provide reference to the original. The
  research that the library actively cites and draws upon is catalogued in the
  resources directory. In general however the library is a fast-moving target and
  lapses of documentation may happen. This is a solo effort at present, and I ask
  that you bring matters to my attention if you notice any lapses in attribution
  or documentation. I will be migrating this project to sourcehut in the near
  future, and mailing lists will be established for this purpose among others --
  in the meantime, submit an issue at the project's issue tracker and I will be
  sure to respond in a timely manner.
- The current documentation is incomplete, and will be refined as the library
  reaches API stability. AI-generated documentation can sometimes be of
  sufficient quality such that only human editorial review is required; in many
  cases however it is a poor substitute for human-authored documentation. The
  aspired standard of the library is human authorship, and at the very least human
  review and editorial authority on all documentary artifacts.
- The chosen format for agda modules is literate markdown, hence in the near
  future I will provide frontmatter that details various metadata attributes per
  module, the relevant marker here being an explicit signoff that verifies the
  module has been reviewed and approved by a human author. I make no binding
  claim to accuracy in the absence of an explicit signoff. Informally I believe
  that many described results (having preceded the use of AI assistance) are
  correct. In particular much of the Core.\* namespace is my own original work,
  or drawn from known efforts of similar libraries and explicitly credited,
  though I do work to continuously audit the work of the library as a whole.
- One tool I plan to develop will assist here by scanning other libraries for
  similar proofs, flagging candidates for human review and citation confirmation
  where provenance is otherwise uncertain. Another feature will do the same for
  the citation resources vendored in the project, scrutinizing and comparing text
  to formalized results to discover results which arose independently or at worst
  had lapses of citation. The standard will be: where a construction matches one
  in a published artifact and its independent provenance cannot be attested by
  the human author's explicit records, attribution defaults to the published
  work, and a citation will be made regardless.

I welcome any corrections or suggestions for improvement in this regard; I
myself have reassessed my approach to the use of AI research several times, and
will continue to do so as the landscape evolves. Transparency and
accountability are important to me, and I do not intend to compromise on those
principles. The ideas in this domain are thrilling, and everyone deserves to be
duly recognized for their contributions to the developing field of univalent
mathematics.

## A short historical commentary on AI usage and methodology in Kitcat

I began to use AI assistance starting from Opus 4.6 in late 2025 after being
acquainted with its use during a prior consultancy to assist with their
endeavors. When I have used it to assist with code generation, this was done in
the belief that AI use is only sound when granted a strong initial foundation
by human-authored definitions and data structures. My belief is that when a
fruitful foundation has been provided, many results depart naturally and
mechanically.

I admire Grothendieck's view that the best proofs are those which are
"trivial" in the sense that they follow naturally from the definitions at each
step. Such circumstances are the ones which in my experience facilitate the
best and most deterministic results from AI-assisted research. I have
constantly sought clarity and elegance in the structures of the library, and
believe my success with AI-assisted code generation is a direct result of the
care and attention I have directed in this regard.

There is a point to which one must retreat from extensive use of such tools,
which I became progressively convinced of by the latter half of 2026. There is
no replacement for direct involvement in proof assistant coding especially. Not
only do AI tools tend to undermine one's ability to oversee the development and
articulation of results, reducing one's ability to gain insight from the nuts
and bolts of proofs, they additionally threaten to make one dependent on their
use. Furthermore, given that the frontier is in general the most competent at
proof-assistant tasks, this presents a non-trivial adversarial risk because
such models are not developed and operated by actors with an uncontroversial
reputation for transparency, fairness, and accountability, much as obtains with
various actors within the proprietary software industry as a whole. This
presents a certain tension which measures must be taken to mitigate. Human
review and signoff, together with retaining developmental initiative, are the
principal such measures. The proliferation of alternative vendors and locally
hosted models is a positive development in this regard, and my efforts are
directed to meet them as they arrive.

For these reasons I have sought to proactively regulate my use of AI after my
initial period of experiment, and have since renewed my focus on direct
engagement with the codebase and its development.

## AI-assisted contributions

Although Kitcat has no additional contributors at the time of writing, I can
only expect that others follow the same spirit outlined in this policy document.
Contributors to the library are expected to be able to explain and justify their
constructions and results, and to be able to provide a clear account of their
development.

## The Euler agentic suite

I am working to explicitly codify the procedures by which AI usage is
regulated in Kitcat via a suite of agentic context management and skill
infrastructure informed by my experience. To the extent that I will continue to
use AI assistance, it will be for trivial chore-related maintenance tasks and
for low-hanging-fruit probative work, which will serve as raw material for
technical clarification and elaboration of personally authored proofs and
theoretical developments.

Therefore I decided to develop a suite of tools to assist with the development
of the library by forking the [feynman project's](https://www.feynman.is) skill
and agentic context suite (MIT license) as its basis. This is an experiment in
agentic context management for proof assistants in general, and is intended to
provide a framework that maximizes the benefits of AI usage, while centering
human users both philosophically and in practice. I maintain that the strongest
results achievable with such methods require this perspective.

I will develop the Euler suite using Kitcat as a testbed for development to
demonstrate the viability of the methodology it encodes with practical
evidence. Once such is suitably established, an effort will be undertaken to
make it portable across different proof assistants (such as Rocq or Lean), and
also deployable to your agentic harness of choice.

That being said, the current fork is at a very preliminary stage, and is not
yet ready to be used for any serious work. I will be working to develop it in
the coming months, and it will not take priority over my actual research
efforts.

## Conclusion

At present I have a more liberal policy on AI usage compared to some projects
in this space. I do however believe that the attempt to inject nuance into the
debate is worthwhile. It seems beyond doubt that the potential unlocked by LLMs
holds great promise, and I furthermore believe that it would be irresponsible
for those most capable of judgment in mathematical affairs to abdicate taking a
strong, and indeed, leading role in helping to decide the proper use of such
tools in mathematical practice by a blanket rejective stance.

Such a stance, I conjecture, has the practical effect of ensuring that the only
actors who may knowledgeably discuss the use of such tools in mathematical
practice are those with the least affinity or accountability towards the
community of mathematicians writ large; the danger is those actors will thereby
be able to progressively appropriate mathematical results beyond
mathematicians' ability to check the development undertaken at their
community's expense. If mathematics is fundamentally a social activity, as the
constructive side believes, the present moment expresses a political struggle
over mathematics itself in no uncertain terms.

I believe the most powerful counter to this dilemma is to wager that any
approach that improves the ability of human oversight and agency in the use of
AI tools will also necessarily coincide with the most effective use of AI tools
overall; that the road to challenge the dangers its irresponsible use poses is
to show concretely that a better road which does not alienate humans from their
intellectual faculties is not only possible, but more compelling than any
alternative. I am confident that the increasing sophistication of AI models
will go on to vindicate this perspective if we can build the infrastructure and
practices to support development in such a direction.

My hypothesis is simple: To the extent that AI agents parallel the competence
of human users, the effective means of augmenting their capacities will
coincide with their ability to augment the ones of human beings. Artifacts that
are inscrutable to human interlocutors undermine the connection between
intentions and results for both human and AI reasoners alike. Human beings were
capable of writing slop long before AI came in the picture, with similar
results that affect the accessibility and reliability of constructions as
exhibited in so-called technical or conceptual debt arising from the unforeseen
consequences of technical decisions.

The case can be made that the history of scientific and mathematical practice
corroborates that complexity does not always correlate with clarity or
efficient technique. Witness the trials of Roman-era long division, whose
training took a lifetime and for which more effective methods are routinely
practiced by literal children in the modern era. Likewise the techniques of
Ptolemaic astronomy, which were so complex that they were only accessible to a
small group, and whose reformulation was critical to the development of entire
domains of mathematical and scientific inquiry. AI could be used to reestablish
the former state of affairs, or to pioneer the techniques that simplify the
subject so as to democratize its practice (and in so doing further the
discovery of similar results). The choice of which occurs is up to us.

To the extent that AI will be fruitful for the development of science and
mathematics in general, it will be by making its practice more and not less
comprehensible to human and AI reasoners alike. Only in such a circumstance
will any sort of reasoner be able to utilize their efforts as a reliable
foundation for further work. It is with that resolve that I seek to demonstrate
this perspective by example. This endeavor participates in a larger effort to
develop sound regulatory and ethical frameworks for the use of AI more
generally. I do not claim that the present policy addresses all concerns, but I
do hope that it serves as a useful entry into the wider conversation about the
responsible and ethical use of such tools, which in my opinion can only reach a
comprehensive resolution through democratically determined public policy.
