# Research provenance, documentation, and AI policy

_by Lane Biocini_

Written and edited by hand, and last updated on 2026-07-04.

This repository is developed with substantial AI assistance under my direction
and review, and aims to be an exemplar of transparent AI-assisted mathematics
research. Truth be told, I'm still figuring out what that means. As such, I see
this project in part as a living entry into the conversation about ethical
practice in this domain. Here are the relevant factors I consider, and a
description of my current approach:

- I will make every effort to ensure that the library is properly documented
  and that authorship of ideas is correctly attributed; particularly because many
  of the ideas explored in this library are my authentic and original work.
  Autogenerated proofs carry the danger of drawing upon work that is not properly
  credited, and may be difficult to trace back to its original source. Just the
  same as I would expect credit for my own ideas, it is imperative that the work
  that inspired me and which I have built on the shoulders of is recognized and
  credited appropriately.
- I intend to be clear when a given construction is derived from a specific
  source, and will provide reference to the original. The research that the
  library actively cites and draws upon is catalogued in the resources directory.
  In general however the library is a fast-moving target and lapses of
  documentation may happen. This is a solo effort at present, and I ask that you
  bring matters to my attention if you notice any lapses in attribution or
  documentation.
- I will be migrating this project to sourcehut in the near future, and mailing
  lists will be established for this purpose among others -- in the meantime,
  submit an issue request here and I will be sure to respond in a timely manner.
- The current documentation is incomplete, and will be refined as the library
  reaches API stability. AI-generated documentation can sometimes be of
  sufficient quality, in many cases however it is a poor substitute for
  human-authored documentation. The aspirated standard of the library is human
  authorship, or at the very least human review and editorial authority on all
  documentary artifacts.
- The chosen format for agda modules is literate markdown, hence in the near
  future I will provide frontmatter that detail various metadata attributes per
  module, the relevant marker here being an explicit signoff that verifies the
  module has been reviewed and approved by a human author. I make no binding
  claim to accuracy in lieu of an explicit signoff on module contents, though
  informally I believe that many of the results (having preceded the use of AI
  assitance) are correct. In particular much of the Core.\* namespace is my own
  original work, or drawn from known efforts of similar libraries and explicitly
  credited, though I do work to continuously audit the work of the library as a
  whole.

I welcome any corrections or suggestions for improvement in this regard; I
myself have reassessed my approach to the use of AI research several times, and
will continue to do so as the landscape evolves. Transparency and
accountability are important to me, and I do not intend to compromise on those
principles. The ideas in this domain are thrilling, and everyone deserves to be
duly recognized for their contributions to the developing field of univalent
mathematics.

## A short historical commentary on AI usage and methodology in Kitcat

I began to use AI assistance starting from Opus 4.6 in late 2025 after being
acquainted with its use during a prior consultancy to assist with their
endeavors. When I have used it to assist with code generation, this was done in
the belief that AI use is only sound when granted a strong initial foundation
by human-authored definitions and data structures. My belief is that when a
frutiful foundation has been provided, many results depart naturally and
mechanically.

I have admired Grothendieck's attitude to mathematics that the best proofs are
those which are "trivial" in the sense that they follow naturally from the
definitions at each step. Such circumstances are the ones which in my
experience facilitate the best and most deterministic results from AI assisted
research. I have constantly sought clarity and elegance in the structures of
the library, and believe that my success in AI-assisted code generation is a
direct result of the care and attention I have directed in this regard.

There is however a point to which one must retreat from extensive use of such
tools, which I became progressively convinced of approaching August 2026. I
regard that there is no replacement for direct involvement in coding in proof
assistants especially. Not only does the use of AI tools tend to undermine
one's ability to oversee the development and articulation of their own results,
reducing one's own ability to gain insight from the nuts and bolts of proofs,
they additionally threaten to make one dependent on their use. Furthermore,
given that the frontier is in general the most competent at proof-assistant
tasks, this presents a non-trivial adversarial risk because such models are not
developed and operated by actors with an uncontroversial reputation for
transparency, fairness, and accountability.

I have therefore sought to proactively regulate my use of AI after my period of
experiment spanning up to the end of July 2026, and have since renewed my focus
on direct engagement with the codebase and its development. I am working to
explicitly codify the procedures from which AI usage is regulated in kitcat via
a suite of agentic context management and skill infrastructure informed by my
experience.

## The Euler agentic suite

I decided to develop a suite of tools to assist with the development of the
library forking the [feynman project's](https://www.feynman.is) tooling as the
basis. This is an experiment in agentic context management for proof assistants
in general, and is intended to provide a framework that maximizes the benefits
of AI usage, while centering human users both philosophically and in practice.
I regard that the strongest results achievable with such methods necessitate
this perspective.

I will develop euler suite using kitcat iself as a testbed for development to
demonstrate the viability of the methodology it encodes with practical
evidence. Once such is suitably established, an effort will be undertaken to
make it portable across different proof assistants (such as Rocq or Lean), and
also deployable to your agentic harness of choice.

That being said, the current fork is at a very preliminary stage, and is not
yet ready to be used for any serious work. I will be working to develop it in
the coming months.

## Conclusion

My explicit belief is that any approach that improves the ability of human
oversight and agency in the use of AI tools will also necessarily coincide with
the most effective use of AI tools overall, and I am confident that the
incresaing sophistication of AI models will go on to vindicate this
perspective. My hypothesis is simple: To the extent that AI agents parallel the
competence of human users, the effective means of augmenting their capacities
will coincide with their ability to augment the ones of human beings. Artifacts
that are inscruitable to human interlocutors undermine the connection between
intentions and results for both human and AI reasoners. Human beings were
capable of writing slop long before AI came in the picture, with similar
results that affect the accessibility and reliability of constructions as
exhibited in so-called technical debt. The history of scientific and
mathematical practice also corroborates that technical complexity does not
always correlate with clarity or efficient techniques (witness the trials of
Roman-era long division, whose training took a lifetime and for which more
effective techniques are routinely practiced by children of the modern era).

To the extent that AI will be fruitful for the development of science and
mathematics in general, it will be by making its practice more and not less
comprehensible to human and AI reasoners alike, for only in such a circumstance
will any sort of reasoner be able utilize their efforts as a reliable
foundation for further work.
