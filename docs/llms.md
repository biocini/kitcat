# LLM policy, research provenance, and commentary

_by Lane Biocini_

Written and edited by hand on 2026-07-05, and last updated on 2026-11-11. See
the appendix for a changelog.

## Introduction

This repository is developed with some LLM assistance under my personal direction
and review; in general it aims to be an exemplar of transparent LLM-assisted
mathematics research. I'm still figuring out what that means, although I have
trended towards conservative policies on LLM usage. Since the time this document
was originally written, this has shifted towards a solely advisory role, and that
in a limited capacity as I've found it less and less useful as time has moved on.

The library began as my own informal blackboard, though I always had broader
aspirations for it. I expect that there is some attribution debt to clear as I
polish it for wider publication and use. The current documentation is
incomplete, and will be refined as the library reaches API stability (indeed I
plan to rewrite some parts entirely).

This is a solo effort at present, and I ask that you bring relevant matters to my
attention. Submit an issue to the project's issue tracker and I will be sure to
respond in a timely manner. I welcome any corrections or suggestions for
improvement in this policy or otherwise for the library. With regard to the use
of generative models, I have myself reassessed my approach to the use of LLM
research several times, and will continue to do so as the landscape evolves.

Although Kitcat has no additional contributors at the time of writing, I can
only expect that others follow the same policies and perspectives outlined in
this policy document. In general contributors to the library are expected to be
able to explain and justify their constructions and results, and to be able to
provide a clear account of their intended contributions and their conformity
with the principles elaborated within this document.

## Citation & Documentation

Kitcat must not compromise on proper documentation and attribution for
intellectual development, particularly because many ideas explored in this
library are my authentic and original work, but more than the imperative to be
consistent if I expect credit for my own work, it is of great scientific
interest for the history of ideas to be known, and to uphold the standard that
those responsible for their genesis be able to account for all aspects of their
development independently of an ethical imperative to so.

1. When a given construction draws upon a specific source, citation to the
   original will be provided.
2. Research that the library actively cites and draws upon is catalogued in the
   resources directory, which is maintained with some LLM assistance. This is
   so that an agent working in this repo has a canonical store regarding the
   constructions explored therein. It is intended to ensure uniformity UX in
   this regard.

Clerical duties such as the (2) indicates ought to be an inoffensive use for
generative LLM capacities, provided that at minimum the guidelines such as proposed
by [this
author](https://www.williamjbowman.com/blog/2026/03/24/please-don-t-generate-your-bibliography/),
are abided by.

### Documentary prose

LLM-generated documentation can sometimes be of sufficient quality such that
only human editorial review is required; in most cases however it is a poor
substitute for human-authored documentation. Hand-written prose is the expected
standard.

## LLM-assisted proofs

Autogenerated proofs carry the danger of drawing upon work that is not properly
credited, and may be difficult to trace back to its original source. In addition
they are often inelegant, and their articulation cannot be counted on at all for original constructions. The level of guidance and review needed for quality results
is involved enough that the overhead of realizing the work via LLMs exceeds the
benefits one would get from directly crafting the intended construction. Such
depth of involvement also imbues the creator with indelible knowledge regarding
their work, which comes in handy for future endeavors. Given that much of the
work pursued in this repository is exploratory and speculative in nature, this
policy applies to the the central work that will be done here.

For this reason, Kitcat will not accept LLM-generated contributions in anything
but controlled and exceptional circumstances. Particularly it will be reserved
for when the structure and implementation of results is mechanical in nature,
i.e. when its shape can be strictly circumscribed by an intelligently curated
architecture. There can be a role for this in the implementation of results
whose presentation and taxonomy is already well organized within conventional
mathematical practice.

## A short historical commentary on LLM usage and methodology in Kitcat

I began to use LLM assistance starting from Opus 4.6 in late 2025 after being
acquainted with its use during a prior consultancy to assist with their
endeavors. When I have used it to assist with code generation, this was done in
the initial belief that LLM use is only sound when granted a strong initial
foundation by human-authored definitions and data structures. My belief is that
when a fruitful foundation has been provided, many results depart naturally and
mechanically. There is a point to which one must retreat from extensive use of
such tools, which I became progressively convinced of by the latter half of 2026.

A scientific approach is required to determine the correct methodology for
LLM-assisted research in mathematics. There is certainly no replacement for
direct involvement in proof assistant code especially. Not only does the
current state of LLM tools tend to undermine one's ability to oversee the
development and articulation of results, reducing one's ability to gain insight
from the nuts and bolts of proofs, they additionally threaten to make one
dependent on their use, which is much worse.

Thus I have sought to proactively regulate my use of LLM after my initial
period of experiment, and have since renewed my focus on direct engagement with
the codebase and its development. In August 2026 I made my decision to halt any
use of LLM assistance, and began to rewrite the codebase in its entirety by
hand in order to provide an ironclad guarantee that all results can be
understood and accounted for by me personally, and thus I can give an honest
representation that they may be relied upon by others without concern.

_This rewrite is currently ongoing, and this statement will be updated upon its completion._

## An agentic suite?

More speculatively, I have an interest in explicitly codifying procedures by
which LLM usage is regulated in Kitcat via a suite of agentic context
management and skill infrastructure informed by my research and experience with
such methods. LLMs have well motivated uses, for example the tasks like:

- Trivial chore-related maintenance tasks
- Mechanical probative work or otherwise generated raw material for technical clarification for personally, such as generation of counterexamples

Ideally one should be able to provide a methodology for AI workflows that
maximizes their benefits while centering human users both philosophically and
in practice. Further, there are many indicators from the first wave of AI
adoption facilitated by LLMs that lead one to suspect that the most effective
techniques of AI assisted work will be ones that enable greater human involvement
and oversight over the performed work.

Interactive modalities are therefore of particular interest in pursuit of
granular and distributed automation methodolgies that can be facilitated by AI
tools to counter the monolothic concierge approaches pioneered in this first
wave of LLM-driven adoption.

The most trivial and mechanical tasks are eminent tasks to be delegated to
machines; in general usage should obey the heuristic that LLM usage enables
them to be _more_ and not less comprehensible to people.

This stance does not require any particular anthropmorphism, rather it departs
from the methodological stance that constructions which have the quality of
being eminently understandable by people and machines alike represent the best
examples of mathematical practice.

I will be working to develop this suite in the coming months, but it will not
take priority over more pressing research efforts.

## Commentary and philosophical remarks

It seems beyond doubt that potentials unlocked by LLMs and related technologies
hold some promise. At present I have a more liberal policy on LLM usage
compared to some projects in this space, and the attempt to inject nuance into
the debate is worthwhile.

Indeed, I think it would be irresponsible for those most capable of judgment in
mathematical affairs to abdicate taking a strong, and indeed, leading role in
helping to decide the proper use of such tools in mathematical practice by a
blanket rejective stance. Since the first draft of this this document this
issue has only become more urgent to address. The danger I envisioned was that
abstinence has the practical effect of ensuring that the only actors who may
knowledgeably discuss the use of such tools in mathematical practice are those
with the least affinity or accountability towards the community of
mathematicians writ large. Like in the history of various other domains it may
be possible to infilitrate both formal and informal settings to advance
extractive and exploitative outcomes for communal endeavors.

I believe the most powerful counter to this circumstance is to pose an
alternative vision and to demonstrate its superiority in practice. The road to
challenge the dangers confronting us with current practice consists in showing
a better methodology which does not alienate humans from their intellectual
faculties is not only possible, but more compelling than any alternative. Such
a vision ought to wager any approach that improves the ability of human
oversight and agency in the use of LLM tools must also necessarily coincide
with the most effective use of such tools overall.

I feel confident that the increasing sophistication of all classes of reasoning
models will go on to vindicate this perspective, so long as we can build the infrastructure and practices to support development in such a direction.

## Conclusion

I do not claim that the present policy addresses all concerns, but I submit it
as this project's entry into the wider conversation about the responsible and
ethical use of AI tools, which in my opinion can only reach an ultimate
resolution when all efforts in this domain are accountable to democratically
determined public policy.
